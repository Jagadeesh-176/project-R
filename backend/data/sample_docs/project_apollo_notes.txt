Project Apollo — Engineering Notes (Internal)

Overview
Project Apollo is the codename for Acme Robotics' next-generation warehouse
picking arm, targeting a Q2 2027 pilot with two logistics partners. The arm
uses a redesigned gripper (internally called "Gripper Mk4") that can handle
irregularly shaped packages up to 4kg.

Key Decisions (from the 2026-08-14 design review)
- The team chose a vision-guided grasping approach over pure force-feedback,
  using a stereo camera pair mounted above the picking cell.
- Gripper Mk4 will use a hybrid vacuum + two-finger design rather than a
  vacuum-only design, after vacuum-only testing showed a 12% failure rate on
  soft-sided packages.
- Firmware for the arm's motion controller will be written in Rust, replacing
  the C++ codebase used in Project Icarus (the previous-generation arm).

Open Risks
- Camera calibration drifts by roughly 2mm after 8 hours of continuous
  operation; the team is investigating a self-calibration routine that runs
  during idle periods.
- Supplier lead time for the Mk4 gripper's force sensors is currently 14
  weeks, which is longer than the project's buffer allows.

Next Milestone
A working prototype of Gripper Mk4 is due for internal demo on 2026-11-20.
